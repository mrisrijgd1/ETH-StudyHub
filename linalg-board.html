What Prof. Halbeisen teaches vs the script

Prof. Halbeisen teaches from his own notes, not from a script. This page compares his lectures from 16 September to 18 November (his §0 to the start of §10) with Script EN 2023: what is the same, what he adds, which definitions differ, which letters differ, and what that means for the exam.

🌧
Bad day? Read only “The short answer” and the table of definitions that differ. Five minutes, and you know the traps. That counts.
You're done when
you can say, for each of the five definitions in section 2, what you would write in his exam, and you can translate his letters (section 3) into the script's without thinking.
Time
About 20 minutes. Come back when a new lecture section starts.

The short answer. The mathematics is the same: everything on his board is in the script or in our lessons, and our lessons already follow his order. Three things are different:

  1. The order. He does the dual space (his §6) before linear maps (§7), and the bidual and the dual map (§9) before kernel, image and rank (§10). The script has the dual space at the very end, in chapter 10.
  2. A few definitions, above all: his rings always have a one, and his change-of-basis matrix goes the other way round. See section 2.
  3. The letters: $\varphi$ instead of $T$, $A_\varphi$ instead of $[T]^B_C$, ${}^tA$ instead of $A^T$, and more. See section 3.

What you do: keep learning from our lessons, in order. They use the script's letters and quote his version in the side notes marked “In the lecture”. In his exam, either notation is fine as long as you say what your letters mean. Add the extras in section 1; they are all in the lessons already.

Source. His lecture notes, read through the typed copies made by the Linear Algebra group (nothing handwritten is shown here), and Script EN 2023 (printed page numbers). Comparison and explanations by StudyHub.

1 · Extras on the board (not or barely in the script)

Each of these is in our lessons, so you need no other source. They matter because his exam will be written from his notes.

Logic and proof methods (his §1)

Truth tables, logical equivalence, quantifiers and how to negate them, then one model proof for each method: direct, contrapositive, induction, contradiction (Cantor), countability, the pigeonhole principle. The script has almost none of this. Expect a question like “rewrite this statement with few logical symbols” or “negate this”. In our lessons: lesson 2 and lesson 3.

The ladder of structures and lean axioms (his §0 and §2)

He climbs semigroup, monoid, group, ring, field, and proves that three lean axioms (a one-sided neutral element and one-sided inverses) already make a group, with an “Unbeispiel” showing that the sides must match. He also builds the field with four elements by hand. In our lessons: lesson 1 and lesson 4; Serie 1 builds $\mathbb F_4$ (Aufgabe 9).

Affine spaces and quotient spaces early (his §5)

Cosets $a+W$, the relation $x\sim y\iff x-y\in W$, and the quotient $V/W$ with “well defined” operations come right after subspaces. The script has them much later (section 3.7). In our lessons: lesson 9; Serie 5 has a sum that is not well defined (Aufgabe 24).

The dual space before linear maps (his §6 and §9)

Functionals, the dual basis with $x_i^*(x_j)=\delta^j_i$, and why the dual set is not a basis in infinite dimension come before general linear maps. In §9 he proves that $V\to V^{**}$, $x\mapsto x^{**}$ with $x^{**}(y^*)=y^*(x)$ is an isomorphism, using a pair of dual bases, and remarks that in infinite dimension it is still injective but no longer surjective. In our lessons: lesson 10 and lesson 14.

The matrix of the dual map is the transpose (his Satz 9.1)

With respect to dual bases, $A_{\varphi^*}={}^t(A_\varphi)$. His worked example: $\varphi(f)=f'$ from polynomials of degree $\le3$ to degree $\le2$, and the functional $f\mapsto\int_0^1f(x)\,dx=1\cdot q_0^*+\tfrac12q_1^*+\tfrac13q_2^*$; then $\varphi^*$ of it is $p_1^*+p_2^*+p_3^*$. Try it: it is a perfect exam question. In our lessons: lesson 14.

Lagrange interpolation as a change of basis (his §7 and §8)

The Lagrange polynomials $L_0,L_1,L_2$ for the points $0,1,2$ are a basis of the polynomials of degree $\le2$. The coordinates of $f$ in this basis are just the values $f(0),f(1),f(2)$, so one change-of-basis matrix turns coefficients into values, and its inverse turns values into coefficients (interpolation). In our lessons: lesson 12 (with an explorer) and lesson 13.

2 · Definitions that differ: what to write in his exam

TopicLecture vs script, and what to write
RingHis rings always have a one; the script's need not. So $2\mathbb Z$ is a ring in the script but not in his lecture. Write “ring with one” when it matters. (lesson 1)
Finite-dimensionalHis: $V$ has a finite basis. Script: $V$ has a finite spanning set. They are equivalent, but in his exam start from a finite basis. (lesson 7)
Linearly independentHis definition: no vector is in the hull of the others; “only the trivial combination gives $0$” is his Satz 4.0. The script starts from the “only the trivial combination” form. They are equivalent; say which one you use. (lesson 6)
$\mathbb Z_m$Serie 0 defines $\mathbb Z_m$ as the remainders $0,\dots,m-1$ with “add, then take the remainder”, without classes. Nothing to make well defined, but associativity needs a proof. (lesson 1)
Change-of-basis matrixHis $B_V$ has the old basis vectors, written in the new basis, as its columns; then $\tilde A_\varphi=B_W\,A_\varphi\,B_V^{-1}$ (his Satz 8.1). The script and our lessons use $Q=[\mathrm{Id}]^{B'}_B$, the new vectors in the old basis, and $[T]_{B'}=Q^{-1}[T]_BQ$. Same result, because $B_V=Q^{-1}$. Write which matrix you mean in one sentence before you use it. (lesson 13)
Rank of a mapHis: $\mathrm{Rang}(\varphi):=\dim\varphi[V]$, the dimension of the image. Same as the script. (lesson 15)

5 · Lecture by lecture

  • 16 Sep §0: semigroups, monoids, groups, rings, fields, with examples.
  • 21 Sep §1: statements, truth tables, equivalences, quantifiers, equality.
  • 23 Sep §1: what a proof is; one model proof per method; countability; pigeonhole.
  • 28 Sep §2: lean group axioms (Satz 2.0); no zero divisors in a field (Satz 2.1); rules in rings (Satz 2.2).
  • 1 Oct §3: the vector space axioms, first rules, the standard examples ($K^n$, matrices, sequences).
  • 2 Oct §4: linear combinations, hull, independence, basis; unique coordinates (Satz 4.1).
  • 5 Oct §4: extending to a basis (Satz 4.3), the Steinitz exchange (Satz 4.5), all bases have the same size (Satz 4.6).
  • 12 Oct §5: subspaces (Lemma 5.0, 5.1), affine spaces $a+W$ (Lemma 5.2, 5.3).
  • 14 Oct §5: the relation $x-y\in W$, its classes are the cosets (Lemma 5.4, 5.5), the quotient space.
  • 19 Oct §5 end, §6 start: functionals; a functional is fixed by its values on a basis (Satz 6.0).
  • 21 Oct §6: the dual space and the dual basis; $\dim V^*=\dim V$ (Satz 6.2).
  • 26 Oct §6 end, §7: linear maps and $\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)$ as a vector space (Satz 7.1).
  • 28 Oct §7: the matrix of a linear map applied to a vector; compositions are linear (Satz 7.2); the matrix product defined by $A_\psi\cdot A_\varphi:=A_{\psi\circ\varphi}$.
  • 2 Nov §7: $V\cong K^n$ (Satz 7.3), maps and matrices (Satz 7.4, Korollar 7.5), $\dim\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)=nm$ (Satz 7.6); Lagrange interpolation (notebooks).
  • 9 Nov §8: one vector in two bases; transition matrices are inverse to each other (Lemma 8.0).
  • 11 Nov §8: Lagrange basis example; the matrix of a map in new bases (Satz 8.1). §9: the bidual, $V\cong V^{**}$ (Satz 9.0).
  • 16 Nov §9: the dual map $\varphi^*$ is linear (Satz 9.1); its matrix is the transpose.
  • 18 Nov §9: the derivative example with $\int_0^1$. §10: image and kernel are subspaces (Satz 10.1, 10.2); rank.

6 · Still to come

§10 has just started. Next, almost certainly: the rank–nullity theorem, then row and column rank, Gaussian elimination and linear systems, then determinants. Our lessons 15–21 already cover these from the script, in that order. Eigenvalues (lesson 21) may only come in Linear Algebra II; the exam covers both semesters, so it is never wasted.
