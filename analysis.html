Analysis 1

Refresher, lessons and tools for the Analysis 1 Basisprüfung.

Contents
  1. Refresher
  2. Lessons
  3. Tools

Einsiedler, following the Einsiedler–Wieser script. Lessons 1–22 cover chapters 1–9.4: up to power series, exp, log, sin and cos, then derivatives, the mean value theorem, convexity, l'Hôpital, the fundamental theorem of calculus, integration techniques, improper integrals, Taylor approximation and differential equations.

Refresher

The current phase: twelve units, one a day, that turn reading into writing. Exam proofs on paper from the frame alone, calculation drills, spot-the-mistake, and every exercise redone on paper. Units appear here as they are ready.

FlashcardsDefinitions, theorems and proof moves, chosen by lesson, in random order.
  1. Unit 1Lessons 1–3: quantifiers, functions, equivalence relations

    Three exam proofs on paper, a spot-the-mistake, the lesson exercises redone and two new ones.

Lessons

  1. Lesson 1The Oath and the Challenge

    Quantifiers: reading and negating sentences built from $\forall$ and $\exists$. This is the grammar of every definition in the course.

  2. Lesson 2The Messenger Guild

    Functions between sets: injective, surjective, bijective, composition, image and preimage. Plus the two proof templates you'll use for the rest of the course.

  3. Lesson 3Great Houses and the Endless Roll Call

    Equivalence relations (sorting a set into classes) and countability (which infinite sets can be listed), ending with Cantor's diagonal argument.

  4. Lesson 4The Siege Engineer's Three Weapons

    Implication, the three proof methods (direct, contrapositive, contradiction), induction, and the 16 axioms of $\mathbb R$, with a deep dive into the completeness axiom.

  5. Lesson 5The Quarter-Turn Spell

    Complex numbers, the absolute value and the triangle inequality, and open and closed sets.

  6. Lesson 6The Lowest Ceiling

    Maximum and supremum, minimum and infimum, the consequences of completeness (Archimedes, density of ℚ, nested intervals, uncountability of ℝ), and accumulation points (Häufungspunkte).

  7. Lesson 7The Unbroken Road

    Continuity with ε and δ, the rules that save you from ε-δ work, and the intermediate value theorem with its proof.

  8. Lesson 8The Walled Garden

    Inverse functions and $n$-th roots, then the three promises a closed, bounded interval makes to every continuous function: it is bounded, it attains its maximum and minimum, and it is uniformly continuous.

  9. Lesson 9Tiles and Canopies

    What an integral actually is: squeeze a function between step functions from below and from above, and call it integrable when the two sides can be pushed together. Then the 7-point exam proof that every monotone function is integrable.

  10. Lesson 10The Measure of Distance

    Chapter 5: what “distance” means in general (norms and metrics), what it means for a sequence to converge, and how continuity can be tested with sequences. This is where class is right now.

  11. Lesson 11The Climber and the Tide

    The first half of chapter 6: the sandwich lemma, why a bounded monotone sequence must converge, and the limit superior and limit inferior, which tell you where a sequence keeps returning even when it doesn't converge.

  12. Lesson 12The Wanderer's Return

    The second half of the sequence theory in chapter 6: every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence (Bolzano–Weierstrass), sequences that run off to $\pm\infty$, and Cauchy sequences, which let you prove convergence without knowing the limit. The Cauchy criterion proof is a full exam question.

  13. Lesson 13Approaching the Gate

    The rest of chapter 6: limits of functions ($\lim_{x\to x_0}f(x)$, one-sided limits, limits at $\infty$), the integral as a limit of Riemann sums, and Landau's $O$ and $o$. After this lesson you're level with class (Monday 2 November).

  14. Lesson 14The Tower over the Lake

    Chapter 7 begins: series, that is, “sums of infinitely many terms”. What it means for such a sum to have a value, the benchmark series (geometric, harmonic, $\sum\frac1{n^p}$), and the tests that decide convergence: comparison, condensation, Leibniz, absolute convergence, and the root and ratio tests. This is where class goes on Thursday 5 November.

  15. Lesson 15The Mapmaker's Apprentices

    Section 7.3: sequences of functions. What it means for functions $f_n$ to converge to a function $f$, why the obvious meaning (“pointwise”) breaks continuity and integrals, and why the stronger meaning (“uniform”) keeps both. Two of its theorems are exam proofs. It also sets up lesson 16, because a power series is a limit of polynomials.

  16. Lesson 16The Spell with Endless Words

    Power series: “polynomials of infinite degree”. Where they converge (the radius of convergence, which is in every exam), how to add, multiply and integrate them, and the most important one of all: the exponential function, with the logarithm, general powers, sine, cosine and $\pi$ coming out of it. Script sections 6.3 and 7.4–7.7.

  17. Lesson 17The Cart's Climb Gauge

    The derivative: what it means to zoom into a graph until it looks straight, the rules that compute it (product, quotient, chain, inverse), and the two theorems that every later chapter stands on, Rolle's theorem and the mean value theorem. Both are exam proofs. Script sections 8.1 and 8.2.1–8.2.2.

  18. Lesson 18Ropes over the Valley

    What the mean value theorem buys you: tests that decide whether a critical point is a maximum or a minimum, convex functions (the graph lies under every chord, and $f'$ is increasing), Jensen's inequality, Cauchy's mean value theorem, and l'Hôpital's rule for limits of the form $\frac00$ and $\frac\infty\infty$. Script sections 8.2.2–8.2.5, with a short tour of 8.3–8.4.

  19. Lesson 19The Ledger and the Hourglass

    The fundamental theorem of calculus: integrating and differentiating undo each other, so $\int_a^bf=F(b)-F(a)$ for any antiderivative $F$. Then the toolbox that turns this into points: integration by parts, substitution, partial fractions, and the trigonometric and half-angle substitutions, drilled on real exam integrals. Script sections 9.1–9.2.

  20. Lesson 20Roads Without End

    Improper integrals: integrals over an infinite interval like $[1,\infty)$, or of a function that blows up at an endpoint, defined as limits of ordinary integrals. When do they converge? The $x^{-\alpha}$ test, the comparison test, absolute convergence, and the integral test that ties improper integrals to series. Then the Gamma function, which extends $n!$ to all positive reals. Script section 9.3.

  21. Lesson 21The Seer of the Carts

    Taylor approximation: the best polynomial of degree $n$ near a point, built from the derivatives at that point. Taylor's theorem with integral remainder (an exam proof) says exactly how far off it is. Then the quick estimates, the Lagrange form of the remainder, and the exam skill “compute the Taylor expansion up to order 4” with all its shortcuts, plus limits and extremum tests as applications. Script section 9.4.

  22. Lesson 22The Rails of the Plain

    Differential equations: equations for an unknown function that involve its derivative. Three types are on every exam: separable equations $y'=f(x)g(y)$, linear equations of first order $y'+f(x)y=g(x)$, and linear equations of second order with constant coefficients $y''+a_1y'+a_0y=g(x)$. For each, a recipe, why it gives all solutions, and how to fit the initial values. Script section 8.5.
