1. Statement sprint (35 min)

A proof question starts with the statement, and graders give points for it alone (often 2 of 7). For each row: write only the claim, with every hypothesis, in at most a minute. Then open the link, compare, and mark the row on your paper:

  • ✓ exact, all hypotheses there;
  • ~ right idea, but a hypothesis or a quantifier was missing or wrong;
  • ✗ couldn't write it.

Then, for every ✓ row, say the proof's “In short” line out loud (it's at the end of each grader's list). If you can't, change the mark to ~.

2. Two proofs under time pressure (20 min)

Pick the two rows of section 1 with the worst marks. (All ✓? Then take the intermediate value theorem and Taylor's theorem with integral remainder: both have come as full exam questions.) Set a timer for 10 minutes per proof and write each one in full, on paper, without looking. Then mark it with the grader's list in its unit.

Ten minutes is about what a 7-point proof gets in a real exam. If you ran out of time, the frame (“what the definitions force”) is the part to learn by heart: with it, the first and last lines write themselves.

3. Mixed set (75 min, exam conditions)

Ten questions from real exams, one from each big topic, none of them used in units 1–11. Work under exam conditions: timer on, phone away, only the official formula sheet. Write the answers on paper first; open each link only to mark yourself afterwards.

  1. Lesson 8, Exercise 3: continuity and the extreme value theorem, true or false (Felder HS22, MC5).
  2. Lesson 11, Exercise 3: limsup and boundedness (Figalli FS26, MC2).
  3. Lesson 14, Exercise 2: for which $\alpha$ does the series converge? (Figalli FS24, MC2).
  4. Lesson 16, Exercise 6: the root test with limsup (PVK mock HS25, MC3).
  5. Lesson 17, Exercise 5: a bijection with a $C^1$ inverse (2018 winter exam, question 8, 4 points).
  6. Lesson 18, Exercise 5: convexity from the mean value theorem (Figalli HS23, question A1, 7 points).
  7. Lesson 19, Exercise 8: two definite integrals (Einsiedler FS22, question 1a and 1c).
  8. Lesson 20, Exercise 3: which improper integral converges? (Figalli FS24, MC10, 2 points).
  9. Lesson 21, Exercise 2: a Taylor expansion to order 4 (Figalli FS24, K3, 2 points).
  10. Lesson 22, Exercise 6: a second-order initial value problem (Figalli FS26, MC13, 4 points).

Marking. Use each exercise's folded solution. A multiple-choice or computation question is right or wrong; for questions 5 and 6, use the points in the solution and be strict (a missing hypothesis costs a point). Note which topics cost you points: you need them in section 4. Seven of ten questions fully right is a good result at this stage.

5. Next: the timed mocks

You are ready for the timed mock exams: real old exams, under exam conditions, then marked. They are the last step before the Basisprüfung, and they show you, better than anything else, where the points are.

  • One mock per sitting, with the real time limit, on paper, only the formula sheet. Mark it the same day or the next, with the official solutions where they exist, or in the chat with your AI and StudyHub.
  • A good order: start with an Einsiedler exam (your professor's style: 2022-01 Einsiedler, HS21 exam, which has official solutions). Then the ones with official solutions (Figalli HS23 and FS24, Felder FS23), then the rest. Keep the PVK HS25 mock exam for last, as the final rehearsal.
  • You have seen some questions before: the lessons used many of them as exercises. That's fine. Do them again under time; what counts now is speed and full sentences.
  • After each mock, every lost point gets a row from section 4. Repair it before the next mock.

6. Tick off and rate yourself

When you're done, tell me in the chat (a few words are enough):

  • how many statements in section 1 got ✓, ~ and ✗;
  • your result in the mixed set, and which topics cost points;
  • which rows of the readiness check you need to repair.

Then we plan the mocks together: which one first, and when. 🎉 That's the whole refresher: twelve units, every lesson redone in writing.
