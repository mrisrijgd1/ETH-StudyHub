Take it with you

Everything on this site in one zip: lessons, flashcards, exam maps, scripts, old exams and formula sheets, in English and German. Use it offline, or give it to Claude and make the lessons your own.

1. Download everything

Download the zip (about 85 MB)

The zip always holds the newest version of the site. Download it again later to get updates.

2. Use it on your computer

  1. Unzip the file. You get a folder called ETH-StudyHub-main.
  2. Open index.html in that folder with your browser. The lessons, flashcards and exam maps all work from there.
  3. Formulas are drawn by a small script loaded from the internet, so keep a connection when you first open a lesson. Without it you see the raw formula code.
  4. The PDF viewers (script and exam viewer) need a tiny local web server, because browsers block them on plain files. Open a terminal in the folder and type python3 -m http.server, then go to http://localhost:8000. Or skip the viewers and open the PDFs directly: the old exams and formula sheets are in the docs folder, the scripts are script.pdf (Analysis) and phys-script.pdf (Physics).

3. Make your own version with Claude

This hub was built for one student’s pace and style: story first, then the formal version, about two lessons a day. Yours may be different. With a Claude project you get the same kind of lessons, shaped around you.

  1. Go to claude.ai, open Projects and create a new project, for example “Analysis 1”.
  2. Add files to the project from the zip. Start small, since what you can upload depends on your plan:
    • the script of your course (script.pdf or phys-script.pdf),
    • a few old exams and the formula sheet from docs,
    • one or two lessons you like (for example lesson-01.html), so Claude sees the format.
  3. Copy the template below into the project’s instructions and fill in the brackets. Delete what doesn’t fit you.
  4. Start a chat in the project, for example: “Make my first lesson”, or “Here is lesson 5 from the hub, rewrite it for how I learn”, or “Quiz me on the definitions from lessons 1 to 3”.
  5. After a few lessons, tell Claude what worked and what didn’t (too long, too easy, more examples). Ask it to update the instructions so the change sticks.

I'm studying [course, e.g. Analysis 1] at ETH for the [exam, e.g. Basisprüfung] on [date]. My goal: [pass / a good grade / understand it deeply].

Main resource: the attached script. Use its notation, definitions, conventions and theorem names as the main version; other ways only as short side notes.

How I learn best: [a picture or story first, then the formal version / definitions and proofs straight away / solving problems and looking things up]. Language: [English / German]; keep the German technical terms in a small glossary.

Time: about [30 minutes / 1-2 hours] on a normal day, [number] lessons per week. On a bad day give me a small minimum task. A missed day is not a restart.

Lessons: build them like the attached example lesson: glossary, the idea, worked examples each in its own box, exercises with step-by-step solutions folded under each one, exam questions from the old exams, and at the end a quick check of how hard it felt. Use formal wording in solutions.

Priorities: let the old exams decide what matters most. Tell me what I can skip and keep a list of everything skipped so nothing gets lost.

Feedback: be direct and concrete. When I send a photo of a handwritten proof, mark it like an exam corrector.

Ask me whenever you need something from me.

Claude can make mistakes, also in maths. When the lecture, the script or an official solution says something different, they win.
