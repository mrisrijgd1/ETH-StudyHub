Linear Algebra I exams

The real Linear Algebra I exams of 2016–2022, problem by problem: what each one asks, which lesson teaches it, and which parts the lessons already work through. At the end, the official mock exam as your final test.

🌧
Bad day? Pick one problem below that is marked “Worked in lesson”, try it for 15 minutes, then read the lesson's solution. That counts. A full exam can wait for a better day.
You're done when
you have (1) written at least three of these exams with the timer, (2) marked each one and redone every problem where you lost more than half the points, and (3) written the official mock last, under exam conditions, and marked it with the official solutions.
Time
180 minutes per exam, plus about 60 to mark it. Nine real exams and the mock; three to five plus the mock is a good target.
When
After lesson 21. Linear Algebra I and II are examined together after the second semester (summer 2027), so these are for the semester break and spring.

How to use this page. Recommended path:

  1. During the lessons: nothing extra. The problems marked “Worked in lesson” are already exercises there.
  2. After lesson 21: write one exam a week on the timed mocks page, newest first: HS21, HS20, FS20, HS18, FS19, HS17, FS18, FS17, HS16. Mark it there; it saves your points.
  3. Problems you lost points on: open the lesson in the list below and redo its section.
  4. Last: the final mock.

Where are the PDFs? The official exam PDFs are not on the public site at the moment. All of them, and more than 50 older Linear Algebra exams, are in the private StudyHub library: with the StudyHub connector, ask your AI “start a Linear Algebra mock” or “show me HS20 problem 5”. It gives hints one at a time and marks your work against the official solution where there is one.

Source. Problem lists and points are read from the official exam sheets (ETH D-MATH, Prof. Pink and Dr. Akka). The short English descriptions are written by StudyHub; the exams themselves are in German. None of these exams has an official solution except the mock.

What every exam looks like

180 minutes, about 100 points, no notes and no calculator. Part 1 is a true/false or multiple-choice block (20–40 points, no reasons needed). Then four to six problems; most open with a definition worth 1–4 points, then a computation and a proof. The plan and exam map sorts the same problems by topic.

The final mock

Dr. Akka's official mock exam, HS20 (102 points, 180 minutes, with official solutions). It is the only Linear Algebra I exam with an official solution, which is why it comes last: you can mark it exactly, and its problems 4–7 are not solved anywhere on the site, so they stay fresh (the lessons only quote a few of its true/false statements).

  1. Write it after at least three of the exams above, on a day with 3 free hours.
  2. Exam conditions: paper, no notes, no calculator, phone away. Start the timer on the timed mocks page and choose “HS20 official mock exam”.
  3. Mark it with the official solutions (through your AI and the connector while the PDFs are offline). Enter the points per problem; the page keeps your score.
  4. The mock also has three bonus questions. They are good warm-up for the next day.

Parts: true/false (28) · short answers (16) · prove or disprove (12) · change of basis and the matrix of a map (12) · complements, and how many over $\mathbb F_2$ (14) · rank of a product (14) · a determinant (6).

Combined Linear Algebra I+II exams

Your real exam will be one of these: Linear Algebra I and II together, after the second semester. The three newest (HS24, FS25, FS26) are in the library; about half of each is Linear Algebra II. Their Linear Algebra I questions already appear in the lessons: HS24 problems 3 and 4 and MC 1, 2 and 11 (lessons 10, 14, 16, 20, 21), FS25 problem 4 and MC 6 and 13 (lessons 10, 14, 19), FS26 questions 1, 3 and 5 (lessons 11, 15, 19). Write a full one in the spring, when Linear Algebra II has covered the rest.
